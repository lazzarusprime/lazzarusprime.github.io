1. Intervals

An interval is the distance between two notes, measured in semitones (half steps). Every scale, chord, and melody is just a pattern of intervals stacked on a root. Learn to hear these by name, not just play them by shape.

Click a pill to hear that interval above the root
Why it matters: Once you can hear "that's a minor 3rd" instead of just "that sounds sad," you can transpose anything into any key on the fly — on any instrument.

2. The Major Scale Formula

Every major scale follows the same pattern of whole (W) and half (H) steps. Memorize the formula once, apply it from any root:

Root W2 W3 H4 W5 W6 W7 HOctave
Click a pill to hear the scale degree
Why it matters: Every mode (Dorian, Mixolydian, etc.) is the exact same seven notes as some major scale — just starting on a different degree. Know this formula and modes stop being a separate thing to memorize.

3. Building Triads & 7th Chords

A triad stacks the 1st, 3rd, and 5th of a scale. Change which 3rd and 5th you use, and the chord's quality changes:

Major: 1 – 3 – 5 Minor: 1 – ♭3 – 5 Diminished: 1 – ♭3 – ♭5 Augmented: 1 – 3 – ♯5

Add one more third on top and you get a 7th chord — the backbone of jazz, funk, and soul:

Maj7: 1‑3‑5‑7 Dom7: 1‑3‑5‑♭7 Min7: 1‑♭3‑5‑♭7

4. Roman Numerals & Diatonic Chords

Harmonize every note of the major scale with a triad and you get seven chords that "belong" to that key — the diatonic chords. Roman numerals name them by scale degree, capital = major, lowercase = minor:

Why it matters: Progressions like I–IV–V or ii–V–I aren't tied to one key — they're a shape. Learn "1‑4‑5" as a relationship and you can play it in any key instantly, which is exactly what happens when a song gets called out in a key you don't know.
